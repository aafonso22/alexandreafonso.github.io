Alexandre Afonso is Associate Professor of Public Policy at the Institute of Public Administration, Faculty of Governance and Global Affairs, Leiden University, where he has worked since 2015. Before joining Leiden, he was Lecturer in Politics in the Department of Political Economy at King's College London (2012–2015), a postdoctoral fellow at the Max Planck Institute for the Study of Societies in Cologne (2011–2012) and a Max Weber Fellow at the European University Institute in Florence (2010–2011). He has also been a visiting scholar at Johns Hopkins University (SAIS) and at the Amsterdam Institute for Advanced Labour Studies. He obtained his PhD in political science from the University of Lausanne in 2010; his thesis received the Faculty Prize.

His research focuses on comparative political economy and public policy: welfare states and labour market policy, industrial relations and social concertation, immigration policy and attitudes towards immigration, and the role of radical right parties and organised interests in policy change. He is the author of *Social Concertation in Times of Austerity* ([Amsterdam University Press, 2013; open access](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/33913)) and of a forthcoming book with Oxford University Press on social protection and the birth of immigration control in Western Europe. His articles have appeared in *Comparative Political Studies*, the *European Journal of Political Research*, the *Journal of European Public Policy*, *Socio-Economic Review*, the *European Political Science Review*, the *Journal of European Social Policy*, the *Journal of Common Market Studies*, *Governance* and the *International Migration Review*, among others. Between 2019 and 2024 he led the NWO Vidi project *The Borders of Equality: Welfare States and Immigration Policies in Comparative and Historical Perspective*.

At Leiden he chairs the Exam Board of the Institute of Public Administration and has served as PhD Dean of the Graduate School of the Faculty of Governance and Global Affairs. He sits on the editorial boards of the *Journal of European Public Policy*, the *Swiss Political Science Review* and *Acta Politica*, and co-leads the SASE research network on the political economy of welfare states and industrial relations.

He grew up in French-speaking Switzerland, holds Swiss and Portuguese citizenship, and works in French, Portuguese, English, Dutch, German and Italian.
