##### Abstract

Switzerland is often treated as an outlier in Europe: outside the European Union, yet obliged to adapt to many European rules without having a say in them. This contribution presents the findings of a comparative research project (2005–2009) that asked whether European integration affects Swiss decision-making differently from small EU member states. Comparing Switzerland with Austria, Belgium and Ireland across strongly Europeanised sectors (free movement of workers, electricity liberalisation, competition law) and weakly Europeanised ones (occupational pensions, unemployment protection), we find no consistent effect of Europeanisation on concertation with organised interests. Its impact is instead mediated by domestic factors: party coalitions, actor strategies, the characteristics of policy sectors and the politicisation of issues. Many Swiss patterns, such as the flanking measures accompanying the free movement of workers, have functional equivalents in member states like Ireland. Non-membership and the indirect nature of Swiss Europeanisation thus explain less than often assumed.
